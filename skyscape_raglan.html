Skyscape Raglan

Top-Down Waistcoat Stitch Raglan Sweater · Size S/M
Seamless · Worked in joined rounds · Right side always facing

At a Glance

  • Finished chest: 39″ (fits bust 32–36″ with 3–7″ ease)
  • Length: about 22″ shoulder to hem · Sleeve: about 17″ underarm to cuff
  • Built top-down in one piece: neckline → raglan yoke → body → sleeves → ribbing
  • No seams, no turning — waistcoat stitch is worked with the right side facing every round

Materials

  • Loops & Threads Skyscape (acrylic blend)
  • Approx. 1,500–1,800 yds total
  • Hook: 6.0 mm (J-10) — body
  • Hook: 5.0 mm (H-8) — ribbing
  • 4 locking stitch markers (raglan corners)
  • 1 marker in a contrast colour (start of round)
  • Tapestry needle
  • Measuring tape
This pattern assumes Skyscape is a medium (#4 / worsted) weight. Check your ball band: if it says #5 bulky or #3 light, the stitch counts below won't match — swatch first and adjust (see Gauge). Waistcoat stitch is yarn-hungry, so buy by yardage, not ball count, and grab an extra ball from the same dye lot.

Gauge

HookStitch4″ × 4″
6.0 mmWaistcoat stitch, in the round14 sts × 16 rounds

Swatch in the round (ch 40, join, work 16 rounds) — waistcoat stitch looks different worked flat because you'd be turning.

Too many stitches per 4″? Go up a hook size. Too few? Go down. Waistcoat stitch is naturally tight; most people need 1–2 hook sizes larger than the ball band suggests.
Gauge matters here — this is a stitch-count pattern. Every 1 st/4″ off changes the chest by about 3″.

Abbreviations & Stitches (US Terms)

  • Ch Chain
  • Sl st Slip stitch
  • Sc Single crochet
  • Hdc Half double crochet
  • WS Waistcoat stitch
  • WS inc 2 WS in the same st
  • WS2tog Waistcoat decrease
  • FPdc Front post double crochet
  • BPdc Back post double crochet
  • Rnd Round
  • St(s) Stitch(es)
  • Rep Repeat
Waistcoat Stitch (WS)

Look at the sc below: each one forms a "V" with two vertical legs. Insert your hook through the centre of the V, between the two legs and under the horizontal strands, so the hook comes out the back of the fabric. Yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over, pull through both loops. It's a sc in a different spot — and it looks like knit stockinette.

Pull your loop up a little taller than you would for a normal sc. If it's a fight to get the hook into the V, your tension is too tight.
Waistcoat Decrease (WS2tog)

Insert hook in the centre of the next V, pull up a loop; insert in the centre of the following V, pull up a loop; yarn over and pull through all 3 loops.

Joining Rounds

Every round: at the end, sl st into the first st, ch 1 (does not count as a st), and work the first WS of the next round into the same st you joined to. Do not turn. Move the start-of-round marker up each round.

Part 1

Neckline Foundation

1

With the 6.0 mm hook, ch 80. Sl st to the first ch to form a ring, making sure the chain is not twisted.

Lay the chain flat and check it isn't twisted before closing the ring. Neck opening ≈ 23″ — it needs to go over your head.
2

Rnd 0: Ch 1, sc in each ch around. Join. 80 sts

3

Place markers. Counting from the first st of the round, mark the 4 raglan "corner" stitches:

Part 2

Raglan Yoke

There are three kinds of yoke rounds. Follow the chart below to see which one each round is.

Full increase round (+8 sts)
*WS in corner st, WS inc in next st, WS to 1 st before next corner, WS inc in next st*; rep * to * 4 times total. Join.
Body increase round (+4 sts)
WS in corner A, WS inc in next st, WS to 1 st before corner B, WS inc in next st, WS in corner B, WS across sleeve. WS in corner C, WS inc in next st, WS to 1 st before corner D, WS inc in next st, WS in corner D, WS across sleeve to end. Join.
(Only the front and back increase — this gives the body extra width without making the sleeves too wide.)
Plain round (no increase)
WS in each st around. Join.

4

Work Rnds 1–28 following the chart.

204 sts: Back 60 · Front 60 · Sleeves 40 each · 4 corners
Check your count at the end of each round — on the body-increase rounds especially, it's easy to put an increase on a sleeve by mistake.
5

Check yoke depth. Measure straight down from the foundation chain along a raglan line. You want about 7½–8″. If it's short, work plain rounds until it's long enough (stitch counts don't change).

Best check: slip the yoke on. The raglan lines should reach your armpits without pulling.
Part 3

Splitting Body & Sleeves

6

Split round: WS in corner A, WS across 60 back sts, WS in corner B. (62 back sts)

Ch 6, skip the 40 sleeve sts, then WS in corner C, WS across 60 front sts, WS in corner D. (62 front sts)

Ch 6, skip the 40 remaining sleeve sts, then join with a sl st to the first st.

The corner stitches go to the body; the sleeve stitches stay live and you'll come back for them in Part 5. You can remove markers B, C, D — keep the start-of-round marker.
7

Next round: WS across 62 back sts, sc in each of the 6 underarm ch, WS across 62 front sts, sc in each of the 6 underarm ch. Join. 136 sts

There's no V to work into on a chain, so use a regular sc there. From the next round on, they're V's like everything else.
Part 4

Body

8

WS in each st around, joining each round. 136 sts

Continue until the body measures 12″ from the underarm, or 2″ less than your desired finished length.

Every 4 rounds ≈ 1″. For a cropped fit, stop around 8″; for a longer, tunic-style fit, go to 15″+ (add yardage).
Hem Ribbing
9

Switch to the 5.0 mm hook.

Rib Rnd 1: Ch 2 (doesn't count), hdc in each st around. Join. 136 sts

10

Rib Rnds 2–7: Ch 2 (doesn't count), *FPdc around next st, BPdc around next st*; rep around. Join.

Fasten off and weave in the end.

If the ribbing flares out, redo Rib Rnd 1 with 16 hdc2tog spaced evenly (about every 8 sts) for 120 sts.
Part 5

Sleeves (make 2)

11

With the 6.0 mm hook, join yarn at the centre of the underarm chain (between the 3rd and 4th ch), working into the unused side of the chain.

Rnd 1: Ch 1, sc in each of the next 3 ch, WS in each of the 40 sleeve sts, sc in each of the last 3 ch. Join. 46 sts

If you see a small hole where the sleeve meets the underarm, pick up an extra st there to close it, then WS2tog it away on Rnd 2.
12

Rnds 2–6: WS in each st around. 46 sts

13

Decrease round (Rnd 7): WS2tog, WS around to last 2 sts, WS2tog. Join. 44 sts

Work 6 plain rounds, then another decrease round. Repeat this (decrease every 7th round) until you've worked 7 decrease rounds total. 32 sts

Decreases land at the start-of-round — the centre of the underarm — so they stay hidden.
14

Work plain rounds until the sleeve measures 15″ from the underarm, or 2″ less than your desired length.

Try it on with your arm slightly bent — that's the length you'll see when wearing it.
15

Cuff: Switch to the 5.0 mm hook. Work Rib Rnd 1 (hdc around, 32 sts), then Rib Rnds 2–7 (FPdc/BPdc) as for the hem. Fasten off.

16

Repeat Steps 11–15 for the second sleeve.

Part 6

Neckband & Finishing

17

With the 5.0 mm hook, join yarn at a back raglan line, working into the unused side of the foundation chain.

Neck Rnd 1: Ch 2 (doesn't count), hdc in each ch around. Join. 80 sts

18

Neck Rnds 2–4: *FPdc, BPdc*; rep around. Join. Fasten off.

For a wider neckline, skip the neckband and just work 1 round of sc. For a mock-neck, work 8–10 rib rounds instead of 3.
19

Close any gaps at the underarms with the yarn tails. Weave in all ends.

20

Block: Acrylic blends respond best to steam. Lay the sweater flat, hover a steam iron above it (don't press down), pat into shape and let it cool and dry fully before moving it. This relaxes the waistcoat stitch and improves drape.

Fit Adjustments

Want to change…Do this
Chest roomier (≈ +2″)Use 10 underarm ch instead of 6 (body 144 sts). Sleeves start at 50 sts; add 2 more decrease rounds.
Chest closer (≈ −2″)Use 2 underarm ch (body 128 sts). Sleeves start at 42 sts; do 5 decrease rounds.
Deeper armholeAdd plain rounds at the end of the yoke (Step 5).
Wider sleevesChange one or more plain yoke rounds into full-increase rounds (+2 sts per sleeve each time); add matching decreases on the sleeve.
Back rides up / neck pulls backNormal for a top-down raglan without short rows — wear the neckband slightly lower in front, or make the neckband 1–2 rounds deeper.
